# Foundations

[Portfolio](../README.md) · [Architecture](architecture.md) · [Research](research-and-findings.md)

## Central idea

Intelligent software needs a trustworthy environment around it: explicit goals, permitted context, evidence, deterministic controls, human decisions, and records that survive interruption and review.

The projects explore different parts of that environment. Their common direction is to make useful work attributable and inspectable while keeping authority with the people accountable for its effects.

## Principles reflected in the work

| Principle | Practical implication |
|---|---|
| Human authority | A recommendation, access right, or technical capability does not create permission to act. |
| Evidence before conclusion | Preserve support, uncertainty, contradictions, time, and scope with important claims. |
| Deterministic controls | Put permissions, transitions, limits, and action gating in explicit software rules where required. |
| Visible failure | Missing evidence or uncertain state must remain visible rather than becoming an invented success. |
| Bounded execution | Define allowed work, resource ceilings, verification, and stop conditions before execution. |
| Separated review | The producer of work is not the sole authority for approving its correctness or effect. |
| Reproducibility | Bind observations to the actual inputs, method, implementation, and environment used. |
| Portability | Keep the reusable application logic separate from provider and customer-specific choices. |
| Negative evidence | Preserve failed hypotheses and defects so later success does not erase how the result was reached. |
| Independent projects | A useful lesson can inform another effort without silently creating a dependency or changing its approved scope. |

These are requirements and working principles. Their presence in a document is not proof that every corresponding control is implemented or effective.

## Development method

The founding projects use Design for Six Sigma through DMADV: Define the problem, Measure it with suitable methods, Analyze causes and alternatives, Design a bounded solution, and Verify the result.

A phase transition requires its own evidence and human disposition. Finishing an artifact or passing a narrow test does not establish completion of the whole phase. Unresolved prerequisites remain visible until accepted closure or explicit disposition exists.

Small experiments answer specific questions before broader investment. When a defect appears, the work distinguishes routine mechanics from a change to meaning, authority, scientific method, security, or scope. Routine repair must stay within existing authorization; material uncertainty returns to human review.

## Long-term direction

The portfolio explores reusable, governed intelligence rather than unrestricted automation. Potential value includes better evidence handling, more reliable handoffs, safer use of AI development tools, and clearer decisions about which model or system improvements merit further work.

These are product and research objectives. Customer benefit, adoption, market demand, and commercial performance require separate evidence.
